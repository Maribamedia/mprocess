Strategic Memo · On Routing, Not Outreach

Most B2B routing runs one way. We build it both ways.

The market does not lack supply. It does not lack demand. It lacks a routing layer that reads both at once — and matches them through research, not volume.

Capital, talent, supplies and demand do not find each other cleanly. Supply sits with capacity that cannot see the demand. Demand sits with requirements that cannot see the compliant capacity. Most operators pick a side — sell-side outbound, buy-side sourcing — and route in one direction only.

The myoProcess model proved the leverage is in the introduction. Our thesis is narrower: the leverage is in routing supply to demand signals, and demand to compliant supply, in the same motion.

The work is not outreach. It is research — mapping who controls the decision, what their live requirement is, and what compliance constraints sit on both sides. Then it is matching: requirements to compliant capacity. Only then do we route.

Funding is one pillar. Talent is another. Supplies, bids and buyers are the third. All three run on the same two-way routing layer — supply to demand signals, and demand to supply signals — filtered for value, demand and compliant capacity.

Value. Does the introduction make sense for both sides, not just ours? Demand. Is the requirement live, with a decision window and a counterparty who can act? Compliant capacity. Can the supply side actually deliver — legally, operationally and at the standard the buyer expects?

When all three filters clear, we route the signal in whichever direction the demand sits. When one fails, we do not make the introduction. That discipline is the product.

We do not route once and walk away. In every market we operate — US, EU, Asia — supply and demand signals are guaranteed to exist. If the first curated introduction does not reach fulfillment, we route again. We keep routing until fulfillment.

We are not a broker. We are not a headhunter. We are not a lead-gen agency. We are a routing layer that runs in both directions — and does not stop at the first introduction.

Being across the US, EU and Asia means we can read demand signals in one theatre and route compliant capacity from another. US mandates run on US hours from Oklahoma. EU and Asia mandates run through our cross-border desk. Funding, talent, supplies, bids and buyers move across all three.

Because in some markets, one introduction is noise. In others, one introduction — routed in the right direction — changes the direction of a company.
